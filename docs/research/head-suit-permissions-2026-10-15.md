# Free head and suit replacement options

Checked 15 October 2026 (America/Los_Angeles). Research only: no creators contacted, permission obtained, assets restored or app changes made. This complements the earlier media-rights audit.

## Recommended route

Use an original fictional pilot head (or the owner's own likeness with their approval) and a distinctly original exosuit. We can model these ourselves in Blender or procedural Three.js geometry, with no asset purchase. Creating a new mesh is not itself clearance to reproduce Iron Man's protected design or Robert Downey Jr.'s likeness. A recognizable recreation remains a separate rights question even if every vertex is new. The US Copyright Office explains that adaptations may require authorization; CC0 also expressly does not clear others' publicity, privacy or trademark rights. [Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ14.pdf), [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).

A generic science-fiction pilot/exosuit aesthetic can keep the head tracking, wireframe, panel animation and instrumentation without reproducing the distinctive movie assets. This is a design recommendation, not a guarantee that any specific finished design is legally cleared.

## Credible no-cost starting assets

| Option | Verified permission and access | Fit / caveats |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Blender Studio Human Base Meshes](https://www.blender.org/download/demo-files/) | The official demo page lists Human Base Meshes v1.4.1, 49 MB, CC0; public downloads are provided via [Blender's directory](https://download.blender.org/demo/asset-bundles/human-base-meshes/). No Studio subscription required for this bundle. | Best detailed head starting point. Requires sculpting, materials, eyes/hair and web optimization; a base mesh is not a ready-made photoreal portrait. Keep provenance with the imported bundle. |
| [MakeHuman / MPFB](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/about/license.html) | Core assets are CC0; project states no payment, copyright notice or attribution is required. [Export FAQ](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/makehuman/faq/can_i_sell_models_created_with_makehuman.html) expressly allows commercial exported models. | Useful configurable fictional human. Software licence (GPL/AGPL) is distinct from core asset/output permission. Check separate licences for optional community clothing, hair and textures. |
| [Quaternius Animated Mech Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/animatedmech.html) | Official page lists four animated mechs, FBX/OBJ/Blend/glTF, CC0 and free personal/commercial use, with an individual free download. Paid Patreon/all-in-one packs are optional. | More stylized/mechanical than Iron Man. Best ready-made suit-adjacent option or source for permitted kitbashing into a new design. [Animated Robot Pack](https://quaternius.com/packs/animatedrobot.html) is another free CC0 alternative, but deliberately cute rather than cinematic. |

CC0 allows modification and public distribution, including commercial uses, without asking for copyright permission. It does not guarantee clearance of third-party rights. Use the original generic assets as supplied or build an original character; do not reshape them into RDJ/Iron Man and assume the base licence cures that issue.

Do not confuse these free downloads with every Blender Studio asset. The site mixes free and subscriber resources and uses different licences for different files. For example, [Charge's free material library](https://studio.blender.org/projects/charge/?asset=7249) is explicitly free, which does not by itself establish that every Charge character rig is a free download. The Human Base Mesh bundle above has a separate explicit CC0/free route.

## Keeping the old models: permission route

- The [Nathang30 Tony Stark head](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/tony-stark-86c267264a2e4903b8c2bcc1b972cb74) was recorded locally as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The live Sketchfab page could not be reread in this research pass. Ask the creator via the source profile whether the exact public professional portfolio use is covered or request an additional written licence. Preserve attribution/adaptation and share-alike requirements unless separately waived. A creator's permission covers only rights they control.
- The [2iemniak Mark VII suit](https://blendswap.com/blend/6708) listing says CC BY, while its original downloaded notice adds a noncommercial fan-art restriction. Ask the creator to reconcile that conflict and expressly authorize public portfolio use and mesh modifications/distribution. The earlier audit preserves the original evidence.
- For the recognizable Marvel character/suit, Disney's [official licensing FAQ](https://www.disneystudiolicensing.com/) directs Marvel commercial and noncommercial requests in writing to **permissions@marvel.com**. Explain the interactive 3D website, screenshots, source models, modifications, worldwide internet availability, hosting, and any promotion of paid work. Ask whether actor/likeness permission is separately required. No public blanket free permission or guaranteed no-fee grant was found. Do not treat silence as approval.

A zero-budget path exists through original generic designs or the permissively licensed assets above. There is no verified zero-cost permission path in this research for restoring the recognizable film head and suit unchanged.
